Planet Hunter: Lembaran Aktiviti Kelas

Memburu dunia yang bergerak dalam imej sebenar teleskop angkasa SPHEREx NASA

Masa: 30 hingga 40 minit
Tahap: Tingkatan 1 hingga 5
Perlu: telefon atau komputer riba, internet

Laman web: https://slashsly5-rgb.github.io/planet-hunter/   (laman dalam bahasa Inggeris)

Apa yang murid akan belajar

  • Bintang kelihatan tidak bergerak kerana terlalu jauh. Objek dalam Sistem Suria bergerak di langit.
  • Semakin jauh sesuatu objek dari Matahari, semakin perlahan ia bergerak di langit.
  • Sebab "Planet X", jika wujud, sangat sukar ditemui.

Langkah 1: Memburu (10 minit)

Tekan Start the hunt. Dua imej langit yang sama bersilih ganti. Ketik objek yang bergerak. Cuba butang Difference jika sukar.

Markah saya: ______ daripada ______    Objek paling sukar ditemui: ____________________

Langkah 2: Mengukur (15 minit)

Buka Explore, pilih satu objek, kemudian tekan Measure it yourself. Ketik objek itu dalam imej pertama dan imej terakhir. Isikan jadual untuk tiga objek.

ObjekJarak dari Matahari (au)Pergerakan (arka saat)Tempoh masaKelajuan (arka saat sehari)

1 au ialah jarak dari Bumi ke Matahari. Arka saat ialah sudut yang sangat kecil: Bulan purnama selebar kira-kira 1,800 arka saat.

Langkah 3: Fikir (5 minit)

  1. Objek manakah yang paling laju? Yang manakah paling jauh?
  2. Apakah hubungan antara jarak dan kelajuan?
  3. Buka Planet X. Tetapkan jarak kepada 100 au, kemudian 600 au. Apa yang berubah? Bolehkah anda menemuinya?
  4. Mengapakah Planet X belum ditemui?

Panduan jawapan untuk guru

  1. Asteroid seperti Vesta dan Ceres paling laju (beratus-ratus arka saat sehari). Pluto paling jauh dan paling perlahan.
  2. Semakin jauh dari Matahari, semakin perlahan pergerakan di langit.
  3. Pada 600 au, planet simulasi itu hampir tidak bergerak dan terlalu malap untuk dilihat dalam satu imej.
  4. Jika wujud, ia sangat jauh: malap dan bergerak sangat perlahan. SPHEREx merakam lebih satu bilion objek, jadi pencarian memerlukan banyak imej, komputer dan ramai orang.

Nota: semua objek dalam laman ini sudah diketahui. Laman ini tidak menemui Planet X. Planet dalam makmal "Planet X" ialah simulasi; langit di belakangnya ialah imej sebenar.

Planet Hunter: Classroom Activity Sheet

Hunting worlds that move in real images from NASA's SPHEREx space telescope

Time: 30 to 40 minutes
Level: Form 1 to Form 5
You need: a phone or laptop, internet

Website: https://slashsly5-rgb.github.io/planet-hunter/

What students will learn

  • Stars appear not to move because they are so far away. Objects in the Solar System move across the sky.
  • The farther an object is from the Sun, the more slowly it moves across the sky.
  • Why "Planet X", if it exists, is so hard to find.

Step 1: Hunt (10 minutes)

Press Start the hunt. Two images of the same sky flip back and forth. Tap the object that moves. Try the Difference button if it is hard.

My score: ______ of ______    Hardest object to find: ____________________

Step 2: Measure (15 minutes)

Open Explore, choose an object, then press Measure it yourself. Tap the object in the first image and in the last image. Fill in the table for three objects.

ObjectDistance from the Sun (au)Movement (arcseconds)Time between imagesSpeed (arcseconds per day)

1 au is the distance from Earth to the Sun. An arcsecond is a very small angle: the full Moon is about 1,800 arcseconds wide.

Step 3: Think (5 minutes)

  1. Which object is fastest? Which is farthest?
  2. What is the link between distance and speed?
  3. Open Planet X. Set the distance to 100 au, then 600 au. What changes? Can you find it?
  4. Why has Planet X not been found?

Answer guide for teachers

  1. Asteroids such as Vesta and Ceres are fastest (hundreds of arcseconds a day). Pluto is the farthest and the slowest.
  2. The farther from the Sun, the slower the movement across the sky.
  3. At 600 au the simulated planet barely moves and is too faint to see in a single image.
  4. If it exists, it is very far away: faint and very slow. SPHEREx records more than a billion objects, so the search needs many images, computers and many people.

Note: every object on this site is already known. The site has not found Planet X. The planet in the "Planet X" lab is simulated; the sky behind it is a real image.
