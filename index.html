Planet Hunter

Two real photos of the same sky. The stars stay still. One thing moved. Can you spot it?

In 1930, Clyde Tombaugh discovered Pluto by flipping between two photographs just like this. Now it is your turn, with images from NASA's SPHEREx space telescope.

These are known objects. We have not found Planet X.

    So where is Planet X?

    Telescopes like SPHEREx photograph more than a billion objects. No one person can check them all. Computers help, but the search still needs people who know what to look for. Now you do.

    What this can't do

    • It does not search for new objects. Every object here is already known, and we chose and checked each one.
    • It shows small prepared patches of sky, not the whole SPHEREx survey.
    • Brightness is adjusted so faint objects are visible. Brightness on screen is not a scientific measurement.

    Where the images come from

    Every image is a real observation by NASA's SPHEREx mission, from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive. Each object's position was confirmed with NASA JPL Horizons. Images were aligned north-up and brightness-scaled; nothing else was changed.
